# Jason Studio — Prompt System

Everything here feeds Stages 3, 6 and 7. The goal of every scene prompt: the
aged-newsprint paper-diorama look of Jason's Vox style — halftone cutouts,
giant stat numbers, print grain, one hot red accent — never a filmed scene.

Every scene prompt has four blocks in fixed order:
**STYLE BLOCK → SHOT → AUDIO → AVOID.** The style block never changes;
all creative variation lives in the SHOT.

## The look (what the style key shows)

- Aged archival paper, muted map/newsprint texture, print grain, matte
- Palette: Archival Tan, Ink Black, Halftone Gray, Hot Red (strokes,
  underlines, arrows only), Mustard (secondary accent only)
- Black-and-white halftone cutout people with a rough white keyline and an
  offset red stroke behind them (never full-color portraits)
- Giant stat numbers treated as hero characters; condensed bold headline caps
- Torn paper edges, archival photo cards, map pins, red underline swipes
- No glossy 3D, no lens flares

## Niche notes (palette stays the same, props and backgrounds shift)

The niche decides the props and backgrounds; the look never changes.
Starting points, extend for any niche:

| Niche | Backgrounds | Props and cutouts | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| crime | case files, evidence boards, rap sheets, street maps, court dockets | evidence tags, red thread and pins, anonymous halftone cutouts with black eye bars, stamps | cold, procedural, a slow reveal |
| finance | ledgers, stock chart engravings, ticker tape, shipping manifests | giant dollar numbers, coin chips, stacked bills, graph arrows | precise, wry, numbers first |
| politics | front pages, treaty documents, world maps, ballot sheets | distant leader cutouts with black eye bars, stamps, flags as flat shapes | measured, serious, stakes first |
| history | aged maps, archive photographs, timelines, letters | period cutouts as halftone figures, artifacts, route lines | grounded, narrative |
| tech and science | blueprints, circuit traces, lab notebooks, diagrams | device cutouts, process arrows, giant stat numbers | curious, clear, concrete |
| any other | pick backgrounds and props from the SHOT menus below that suit it | halftone cutouts and giant numbers | match the niche, stay newsroom-clear |

Real-people and crime caution: never name or show private individuals or
victims; keep figures as anonymous halftone cutouts with black eye bars,
and keep imagery non-graphic.

## 1. Choose a Style Block (per scene, verbatim)

### A. Flat Parallax (documentary, restrained)

```
Use the attached style sheet as the strict visual system — match its aged-newsprint collage surface, desaturated archival palette with one hot accent, condensed headline caps with giant stat numbers, halftone black-and-white cutout people with rough white keylines and offset accent strokes, and its print-grain finish. Do NOT copy the sheet's layout; it defines the language, not the composition. Layers sit at distinct depths like a paper diorama — true parallax, never glossy 3D. Backgrounds are muted archival fields, varying per clip within this palette. ALERT WASH means the whole frame floods toward the hot accent tone. Motion: spring pop-ups with overshoot, staggered entrances, ticking counters, underline swipes; one slow camera move per clip. Audio: sound design only — paper pops, thwips, stamps, ticks, low newsroom hum. No music. No voice-over.
```

### B. Deep Diorama (cinematic, camera-as-actor)

```
Use the attached style sheet for materials only — aged-print collage textures, halftone black-and-white cutout people with rough keylines and offset accent strokes, giant stat numbers, print grain. Do NOT copy the sheet's layout or its flatness: every clip is a deep 3D paper diorama — cutouts are physical layers separated in real space, strong shallow depth of field, foreground elements crossing close to the lens. The camera is an actor: it flies between layers, orbits, dives, whips, racks focus — one committed cinematic move per clip. Backgrounds change per clip, always within the sheet's palette. ALERT WASH means the entire frame floods to the hot accent tone in one beat. Motion: springs with overshoot, staggered entrances, ticking counters. Audio: sound design only — paper pops, whooshes, stamps, ticks. No music. No voice-over.
```

Why the clauses exist: "materials only / do NOT copy the layout or its
flatness" kills flat 2D output; "physical layers / shallow depth of field /
crossing the lens" forces parallax; "camera is an actor / one move" prevents
mushy drift; "backgrounds change" stops sameness; "ALERT WASH" is a reusable
named beat; "sound design only" locks the audio. Do not delete them.

Use the shift as a tool: Deep → Flat reads as a breath, Flat → Deep as a lift.

## 2. Write the SHOT

Two to five sentences, no headers or bullets, this order:

```
Background: [what + how far + focus state].
MG: [subject cutouts, staggered entrance].
FG: [counter / giant number / label] reading "[TEXT]" ([type tag]).
Camera [ONE move], focus [racking behaviour].
Settle [where].
```

A 10s scene has room for one prominent timed event when it sharpens sync
with the narration — e.g. "at ~5s the tower of cards collapses". Use it
only when it genuinely helps, not on every scene.

**Background** (always tilted in perspective or receding, soft focus):
faded world map · torn ledger page · newspaper column wall · stacked front
pages receding · blueprint grid · stock chart engraving · passport stamp
field · topographic map · index-card wall · shipping manifest · treaty
document · ticker tape wall

**Foreground elements:** cards · torn strips · logo plates · giant stat
number · halftone cutout person · coin chips · arrows · stamps · pins with
thread · folder tabs · ticker tape

**Depth cue (mandatory):** state layers — "at three different depths" ·
"one near the lens, two deep" · "stacked receding into the frame" · "the
nearest whipping past the lens with motion blur"

**Camera — exactly ONE:** push-in · slow drift lateral · dive through a
layer · orbit quarter turn · whip ×2–3, each landing hard · fast lateral
track then hard stop with overshoot · climb alongside a rising element ·
fly low between columns · ride a path like a rail · macro slide with focus
rack

**Settle:** mid-orbit · on the number · on the closing loop · on the
cutout's face · wide on the full diorama · deep, looking up

### Rules

1. One camera move per scene. Never two, never a cut.
2. Always name at least two depths.
3. Always end on "Settle on…".
4. Tag every text element: `(headline)`, `(label)`, `(counter)`,
   `(giant number)`. Numbers are giant set dressing, not captions.
5. Stagger everything — nothing enters simultaneously.
6. Logos only as "attached… flat, unmodified" — never described or invented.
7. Change the background every scene, stay inside the palette. Vary the
   camera move too — never two dives in a row.
8. Present tense, active verbs. No stage-direction tags, no shot numbers.

### Named beats and escalation

- **ALERT WASH** — frame floods to hot accent. One scene per video.
- **STAMP** — a stamp slams in at foreground depth, dust puff
- **TICK-UP** — giant number counts up with overshoot, then settles
- **TEAR** — a paper layer rips to reveal the one behind
- **THREAD PULL** — pins and red thread draw a connection between cutouts
- Freeze before contact · Counter slam · Accelerating loop

Chain scenes by reusing geometry: build the loop, ride the loop, blow past
it. End the video on a held-tension frame.

## 3. Write the AUDIO

`AUDIO: [2–3 sounds, comma separated] — sound design only, no music, no narration.`

Sound mirrors the camera move:

| Move | Sound |
|---|---|
| Dive | submerge whoosh, deep pressure hum |
| Whips | three whip whooshes, pops landing each hit |
| Track + stall | track rumble, stall click, drain hiss |
| Contact / wash | rising air riser, deep sub thud on contact |
| Loop | accelerating ticks, whoosh per lap |
| Tension hold | rubber creak, room tone falling to near silence |

Beds: `low newsroom hum` · `low hum` · `room tone`.

## 4. AVOID blocks (verbatim)

For Flat Parallax:

```
AVOID: no glossy CG 3D, no lens flares, no camera cuts within the clip, no full-color midground portraits (people stay halftone black-and-white), no warped or gibberish text, no invented logos, no watermarks, no simultaneous entrances, no music, no soundtrack, no voice-over, no narration, no lyrics
```

For Deep Diorama:

```
AVOID: no flat single-plane composition, no static locked-off camera, no glossy plastic CG (depth stays papercraft), no full-color midground portraits (people stay halftone black-and-white), no warped or gibberish text, no invented logos, no watermarks, no music, no soundtrack, no voice-over, no narration, no lyrics
```

Add `no UI or glass elements` when the shot involves screens or dashboards.

## Real people (moderation map)

- **Never put a real person's name in a video prompt.** Describe them as
  "the cutout figure", "the distant leader in the red tie". Names are safe
  in the voiceover — it's TTS.
- Keep figures **far from camera**, small in frame, minimal facial detail,
  with a **solid black bar over the eyes**, halftone cutout style. This
  sells the editorial look and defuses likeness refusals.
- Close-up recognizable faces get refused by many engines — go mid-shot or
  full-body.
- Flagged imagery (e.g. mushroom clouds) trips filters — swap the
  silhouette, keep the idea.
- Platforms that push a "photoreal" or "3D render" preset: decline it.

## Worked example — Scene 1 of a 6-scene video

Illustrative only: topic, numbers and script below are placeholders for
shape, not researched facts.

Narration: *"Every year, one country quietly spends more on interest than on its entire military. Here's the thing — nobody voted for that."*

```
Use the attached style sheet for materials only — aged-print collage textures, halftone black-and-white cutout people with rough keylines and offset accent strokes, giant stat numbers, print grain. Do NOT copy the sheet's layout or its flatness: every clip is a deep 3D paper diorama — cutouts are physical layers separated in real space, strong shallow depth of field, foreground elements crossing close to the lens. The camera is an actor: it flies between layers, orbits, dives, whips, racks focus — one committed cinematic move per clip. Backgrounds change per clip, always within the sheet's palette. ALERT WASH means the entire frame floods to the hot accent tone in one beat. Motion: springs with overshoot, staggered entrances, ticking counters. Audio: sound design only — paper pops, whooshes, stamps, ticks. No music. No voice-over.
SHOT:
Background: a torn ledger page tilted away in perspective, deep out of focus.
MG: a halftone cutout of a lone clerk stands small on a stack of ledgers, a red offset stroke behind them, entering first.
FG: a giant stat number reading "$1T" (giant number) drops in close to the lens a beat later; torn strips flutter past at foreground depth.
The counter ticks up with overshoot, and at ~6s a red underline swipes beneath it.
Camera pushes in through the gap beside the number, focus racking from the strips to the clerk.
Settle on the number.
AUDIO: low newsroom hum, soft tick per counter step, paper pop on the underline — sound design only, no music, no narration.
AVOID: no flat single-plane composition, no static locked-off camera, no glossy plastic CG (depth stays papercraft), no full-color midground portraits (people stay halftone black-and-white), no warped or gibberish text, no invented logos, no watermarks, no music, no soundtrack, no voice-over, no narration, no lyrics
```

## Script example (structure, 6 scenes = 1 minute)

Cold open stat → stakes → two evidence beats → the turn → kicker that
reframes Scene 1. Each line is one idea, 18–20 words, numbers spelled out,
no filler.

```
Scene 1  Every year, one country quietly spends more on interest than on
         its entire military. Here's the thing — nobody voted for that.
Scene 2  Debt used to be a footnote. Now interest payments grow faster
         than the economy that has to pay them.
Scene 3  Since twenty twenty, borrowing costs have more than doubled. Every
         new dollar of debt now buys less than it did.
Scene 4  Washington could cut spending or raise taxes. Both are political
         poison, so both keep getting postponed another year.
Scene 5  Here's the twist: postponing is itself a policy. It just hides
         the bill inside everyone's future paycheck.
Scene 6  So the real budget fight isn't about this year. It's about who
         pays for the years that already happened.
```
